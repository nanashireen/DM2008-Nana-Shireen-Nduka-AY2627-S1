*** Reflection

Group: Jocelyn Eddy, Nana-Shireen Nduka and Sahara Ahmed

We wanted to create a system where the user interacts with a pressure sensor to control plants in a virtual garden. Creating a microcosm of reality, enabling the user to experience the circle of life in a very fast time frame without actually affecting the world around them. As the user exerts different levels of pressure on the sensor, different species of flowers grow. As the space becomes more crowded, the older flowers die. Mimicking the fight for resources in the natural world. Utilising an Arduino connected to a pressure sensor, mapping the output through p5.js. We utilised the Web Serial API so that the hardware can be accessed through the ports connected to the laptop. This way, the pressure directly interacted with the p5.js elements. Mathematical functions and L-Systems were implemented to control the flower rendering and its branch generation. As the user presses on the sensor, a random flower starts growing. The level of pressure exerted changes how big the flower initially starts. Additionally, as time passes and the scene gets busier, the older flowers begin to wither and wilt. Until they disappear. This process imitates reality. We chose a dark background to contrast and emphasise the vivid flowers, influenced by the cyber botanical movement. We chose a magical chime noise for when the flower appears to emphasise the futuristic generation of the flowers. This also ensured that the appearance of new flowers was emphasised and more obvious. Especially if the user is visually impaired or prefers audio cues. As each member of our team worked on a different aspect, it was difficult trying to combine them effectively. It resulted in our program lagging, as it was difficult to render the flower generation and the three-dimensional appearance smoothly together. Making the overall experience moderately frustrating. Therefore, if we did this again, we should have combined our separate aspects earlier, leaving more time for troubleshooting.
